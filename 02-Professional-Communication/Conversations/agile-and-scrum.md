# Agile, Scrum & Meeting Vocabulary

---

### Scrum Ceremonies
- **Pronunciation**: "skrum SEH-ruh-moh-neez" · سكرم سيريمونيز
- **Arabic**: طقوس سكرم
- **Definition**: The four structured meetings in the Scrum framework: **Daily Standup** (15 min, what I did / will do / blockers), **Sprint Planning** (what work enters this sprint), **Sprint Review** (demo what was built), and **Sprint Retrospective** (what went well / what to improve).
- **Context**: Daily team collaboration, Agile project management, interview questions about methodology.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "I participate in all Scrum ceremonies and actively contribute to retrospectives with actionable improvement proposals."
  - *Casual*: "Can we bring this up in the retro instead of derailing the standup?"
- **Common Mistake**: Turning the daily standup into a status report for the manager. It's a team synchronization tool — talk to your teammates, not your boss. Keep it under 15 minutes.
- **Related Terms**: Sprint, Backlog, Velocity, Scrum Master, Product Owner

### Cross-functional Team
- **Pronunciation**: "cross FUNK-shun-ul teem" · كروس فَنكشنل تيم
- **Arabic**: فريق متعدد التخصصات
- **Definition**: A team composed of people with different expertise (frontend, backend, ML, design, QA, domain experts) who work together toward a shared goal. The opposite of siloed teams where each department works independently.
- **Context**: Describing team structure in interviews, collaboration anecdotes, organizational discussions.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "I collaborated in a cross-functional team including frontend developers, ML engineers, and domain experts to deliver the computer vision module."
  - *Casual*: "Working cross-functionally helped us ship faster — the designer and I iterated on the UI together instead of throwing mockups over the wall."
- **Common Mistake**: Saying "cross-functional" when you mean "I talked to another team once." True cross-functional work means you're on the same team, in the same standups, working on the same sprint.
- **Related Terms**: Collaboration, Interdisciplinary, T-shaped Skills, Siloed Teams

### "Align on scope upfront"
- **Arabic**: الاتفاق على النطاق مسبقاً
- **Meaning**: Having a clear agreement about what a task/feature includes and excludes *before* investing significant time building it.
- **Context**: Sprint planning, feature kickoffs, freelance project negotiations, open-source PRs.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "It's a much smaller time investment to align on scope upfront than to rewrite a finished draft that doesn't match expectations."
  - *Casual*: "Let's align on scope before you start coding — I don't want you to build the whole admin panel when we only need the user list."
- **Why It Works**: It shows project management maturity. Scope creep kills projects, and proactively preventing it is a senior behavior.
- **Common Mistake**: "Aligning on scope" verbally without writing it down. If it's not documented (even in a Slack message), it's not aligned — it's a future argument.

### Blocker
- **Pronunciation**: "BLOK-er" · بلوكر
- **Arabic**: عائق
- **Definition**: Anything that prevents a team member from making progress on their current task. Raised during daily standups so the team or manager can help unblock it.
- **Context**: Daily standups, sprint boards, status updates, Jira tickets.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "I have a blocker on the payment integration — I need API credentials from the Paymob team before I can proceed with webhook testing."
  - *Casual*: "I'm blocked on this — waiting for DevOps to give me access to the staging database."
- **Common Mistake**: Not raising blockers early enough. Engineers sometimes spend hours trying to solve something alone when a 2-minute conversation with a teammate would unblock them. Raise blockers the moment you realize you can't proceed independently.
- **Related Terms**: Unblock, Impediment, Dependency, Standup

### Stakeholder
- **Pronunciation**: "STAYK-hohl-der" · ستيك هولدر
- **Arabic**: أصحاب المصلحة
- **Definition**: Anyone who has an interest in or is affected by a project's outcome — product managers, clients, end users, executives, other teams. In engineering, "talking to stakeholders" means understanding requirements from the people who will use or pay for what you're building.
- **Context**: Requirements gathering, sprint reviews, project planning, client communication.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "I presented the sprint demo to stakeholders, gathering feedback that shaped the next sprint's priorities."
  - *Casual*: "Who are the stakeholders for this feature? We should loop them in before we finalize the design."
- **Common Mistake**: Treating "stakeholder" as a synonym for "manager." Stakeholders include end users, customer support, sales, legal, and anyone else affected by the product. Engineers who only talk to their manager miss critical requirements.
- **Related Terms**: Requirements, Sprint Review, Product Owner, User Story
