## Definition

A polite, professional phrase used to invite feedback or opinions from colleagues regarding a specific document, code change, or proposal.

## Where you hear it

In pull request comments, Slack messages, or email threads when seeking input.

## Examples

- I have updated the documentation; let me know your thoughts.
- Here is the draft for the new API design, let me know your thoughts.
- The proposal is attached, so let me know your thoughts before Monday.

## Common mistake

Using it when you actually need a formal approval, as this phrase implies a casual request for feedback rather than a required sign-off.

## Don't confuse with

Let me know your thoughts vs. Please approve. 'Let me know your thoughts' invites collaborative feedback and discussion, whereas 'Please approve' is a direct request for a formal sign-off to proceed with the task.

## Say it at work

- I've pushed a quick fix for that bug, let me know your thoughts when you have a second.
- I have attached the initial architecture proposal for the new module; please let me know your thoughts on the proposed structure.
