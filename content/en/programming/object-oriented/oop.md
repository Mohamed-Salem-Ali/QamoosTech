## Definition

Object-oriented programming (OOP) organises code around objects that combine data and the behaviour that works on it, created from classes.

## Where you hear it

In almost every programming course, design discussions, and interviews that ask about the pillars of OOP.

## Examples

- We modelled members and payments as objects, each with its own methods.
- The four pillars of OOP are encapsulation, abstraction, inheritance and polymorphism.

## Common mistake

Forcing everything into classes. Simple data and plain functions are often clearer than a deep class hierarchy.

## Don't confuse with

Functional programming, which builds programs from pure functions and plain data instead of objects that change.

## Say it at work

- Let's keep the design simple: a couple of classes, no deep inheritance.
- OOP fits well here because the entities have behaviour.
