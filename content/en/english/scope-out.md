## Definition

To analyze and define the requirements, goals, and boundaries of a project or task before beginning the actual work. It involves understanding what needs to be built and what is out of scope.

## Where you hear it

During project planning meetings, initial client discovery calls, or when assigning new tasks to a team.

## Examples

- We need to scope out the new feature before we start writing any code.
- Let's spend an hour to scope out the requirements for the upcoming migration.

## Common mistake

Confusing "scoping out" with "scope creep"; the former is a proactive planning step, while the latter is the uncontrolled expansion of a project's requirements during development.
