## Definition

To start planning again from the beginning because the first plan failed.

## Where you hear it

Design and project discussions.

## Examples

- The client rejected the design, so it's back to the drawing board.
- The prototype failed. Back to the drawing board.

## Common mistake

Using it for small problems. A failed test is not "back to the drawing board".

## Don't confuse with

Back to the drawing board is often confused with 'starting from scratch', but while both imply restarting, the former specifically emphasizes that the previous strategy was fundamentally flawed or rejected.

## Say it at work

- The performance metrics are way off, so I guess it's back to the drawing board for our architecture.
- Given the feedback from the security audit, we need to go back to the drawing board regarding our authentication flow.
