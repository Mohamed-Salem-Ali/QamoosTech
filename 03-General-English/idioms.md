# Idioms

---

### "Hit the ground running"
- **Pronunciation**: "hit the ground RUN-ning" · هيت ذا جراوند رانينج
- **Arabic**: بدأ العمل فوراً بكفاءة
- **Meaning**: To start a new role or project and immediately be productive, with minimal ramp-up time.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "Thanks to my experience with NestJS and Prisma, I was able to hit the ground running on the dental clinic SaaS from day one."
  - *Casual*: "The new hire hit the ground running — she shipped her first PR on day two."
- **Common Mistake**: Using it to describe yourself in an interview without evidence. Don't just say "I'll hit the ground running" — explain *why* (prior experience with their stack, domain knowledge, etc.).

---

### "Move the needle"
- **Pronunciation**: "moov the NEE-dul" · موف ذا نيدل
- **Arabic**: أحدث تأثيراً ملموساً
- **Meaning**: To make a noticeable, measurable impact. Often used when discussing whether a task is worth the effort.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "Optimizing the database queries moved the needle on page load times — from 2.3 seconds to 400ms."
  - *Casual*: "Is this refactor actually going to move the needle, or are we just polishing code?"
- **Common Mistake**: Overusing it for trivial improvements. Fixing a typo in the README didn't "move the needle." Reserve it for meaningful, measurable impact.

---

### "Low-hanging fruit"
- **Pronunciation**: "loh HANG-ing froot" · لو هانجينج فروت
- **Arabic**: الثمار السهلة القطف (المهام السهلة ذات الأثر الكبير)
- **Meaning**: Tasks that are easy to complete and deliver quick value — the obvious improvements you should tackle first.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "Before the major refactor, let's pick off the low-hanging fruit — there are three N+1 queries we can fix in an hour."
  - *Casual*: "Adding an index on that column is low-hanging fruit — it'll cut the query time in half."
- **Common Mistake**: Spending all your time on low-hanging fruit and never tackling the hard problems. It's a prioritization tool, not an avoidance strategy.

### "Back to the drawing board"
- **Pronunciation**: "bak tuh the DRAW-ing bord" · باك تو ذا دروينج بورد
- **Arabic**: العودة إلى نقطة البداية
- **Meaning**: Starting over because the current approach failed or was rejected.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "The client rejected the proposed architecture, so we went back to the drawing board and redesigned the data flow."
  - *Casual*: "The migration script corrupted the data — back to the drawing board."
- **Common Mistake**: Using it dramatically for minor setbacks. A failed unit test isn't "back to the drawing board" — a fundamentally flawed architecture is.

---

### "Reinventing the wheel"
- **Pronunciation**: "ree-in-VENT-ing the weel" · ري إنفينتينج ذا ويل
- **Arabic**: إعادة اختراع العجلة
- **Meaning**: Building something from scratch when a well-tested solution already exists.
- **Usage Examples**:
  - *Formal*: "Rather than reinventing the wheel, we adopted Paymob's existing webhook verification library instead of writing our own signature validator."
  - *Casual*: "Don't reinvent the wheel — there's a library for that."
- **Common Mistake**: Using this to shut down all custom solutions. Sometimes existing libraries don't fit your constraints (security, performance, licensing), and building your own is the right call. Know when to build vs. buy.
